## The short version
Mushroom supplements like Lion's Mane may support ADHD through neuroplasticity and anti-inflammatory pathways, but evidence is early and not a replacement for medication. Quality control is critical, and medical consultation is required.

## Why it matters
ADHD affects millions, and many seek complementary options. Understanding the distinct mechanisms of mushrooms (e.g., NGF stimulation vs. dopamine reuptake) helps set realistic expectations and informs safer integration with existing treatments.

## Try this today
- Consult a qualified healthcare provider before adding mushroom supplements to any ADHD treatment plan, especially if taking prescription medications.
- If exploring supplements, prioritize products with third-party testing, verified beta-glucan content, and fruiting body extracts to ensure quality and safety.

## Limitations
The source is an informational article, not a clinical guideline. It contains affiliate links and promotional content for an ADHD app. The scientific evidence cited is largely from animal studies or trials on mild cognitive impairment, not direct ADHD trials. The supplement market is unregulated, and individual responses vary. This summary does not provide medical advice or recommend specific products.
