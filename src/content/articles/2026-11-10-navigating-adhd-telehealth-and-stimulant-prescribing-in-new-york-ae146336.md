## The short version
Telehealth in New York allows for ADHD diagnosis and stimulant prescribing under temporary federal rules expiring in late 2026. Stimulants are Schedule II controlled substances requiring careful screening for heart and substance use history, while non-stimulants offer an alternative for those with existing diagnoses. Platform choices depend on whether a new diagnosis or ongoing management is needed.

## Why it matters
Understanding the legal and medical distinctions between stimulant and non-stimulant ADHD treatments is essential for making informed decisions about telehealth care. The temporary nature of current prescribing rules and the reliance on self-reported history mean that patients must be proactive in disclosing relevant medical details to ensure safe and effective treatment.

## Try this today
- Confirm current New York state telehealth prescribing requirements with a licensed provider before booking an evaluation.
- Disclose full medical history, including cardiovascular conditions and substance use, during the telehealth intake to ensure appropriate medication screening.
- Seek immediate medical attention if experiencing chest pain, heart palpitations, or suicidal thoughts after starting or changing ADHD medication.
