Stimulants, including methylphenidate and amphetamine-based options, are typically first-line treatments because they act within hours. Nonstimulants, such as atomoxetine, guanfacine, and viloxazine, are generally second-line options used when stimulants are contraindicated, poorly tolerated, or ineffective. While stimulants offer rapid relief, they are Schedule II controlled substances with restrictions on refills and potential for dependency. Nonstimulants require weeks to reach full effect and carry risks including suicidal ideation, particularly in younger patients. Telehealth regulations currently allow video visits to prescribe stimulants without a prior in-person exam through December 31, 2026, though state laws vary regarding which providers can prescribe these medications. Patients should rule out other causes of inattention, such as thyroid issues or vitamin D deficiency, and seek immediate medical attention for severe side effects like chest pain or suicidal thoughts."

## The short version
Stimulants are the fast-acting first-line treatment for ADHD, while nonstimulants are slower-acting second-line options. Telehealth prescribing rules are temporary, and individual medical history determines the appropriate medication.

## Why it matters
Understanding the differences in onset time, side effects, and legal restrictions helps patients make informed decisions with their healthcare providers. Awareness of temporary telehealth regulations prevents confusion about access to care, and recognizing serious side effects ensures timely medical intervention.

## Try this today
- Consult a licensed healthcare provider to evaluate symptoms and determine the appropriate medication class based on personal medical history.
- Verify with your pharmacy whether they will fill prescriptions issued via telehealth before committing to an online evaluation service.
- Seek immediate medical attention for severe symptoms such as chest pain, fainting, or new suicidal thoughts while on ADHD medication.

## Limitations
This summary is for informational purposes only and does not constitute medical advice. Telehealth prescribing flexibilities are temporary and may change. Individual responses to medication vary, and specific dosing or treatment plans must be determined by a qualified professional.
