## The short version
Healthy anger processing involves recognizing triggers and using techniques like breathing and CBT rather than suppression or aggressive venting, which can worsen physical and mental health.

## Why it matters
Unprocessed or suppressed anger is associated with higher risks of cardiovascular disease, chronic anxiety, and depression. Learning to regulate anger improves physical health, strengthens relationships, and prevents the escalation of emotional distress into long-term psychological issues.

## Try this today
- Practice immediate grounding techniques such as box breathing or the 5-4-3-2-1 sensory method when anger arises to interrupt the physiological response.
- Keep a brief record of anger triggers and physical sensations to identify personal patterns and clusters over time.
- Consult a qualified mental health professional if anger leads to aggression, lasts for extended periods, or causes significant distress in relationships or work.

## Limitations
This summary is for informational purposes only and does not constitute medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Individual responses to techniques vary, and professional guidance is essential for complex cases involving trauma or co-occurring mental health conditions.
