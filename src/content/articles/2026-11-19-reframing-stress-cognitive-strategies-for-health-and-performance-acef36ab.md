## The short version
Reframing stress as a challenge rather than a threat can improve physical health and performance by altering the brain's physiological response to pressure.

## Why it matters
The mental interpretation of stress significantly influences cardiovascular health, cognitive function, and long-term mortality risk. Understanding this mechanism allows individuals to use cognitive tools to mitigate the physical toll of chronic stress.

## Try this today
- Practice slow, deliberate breathing with extended exhales to reduce acute physiological arousal before attempting cognitive reframing.
- Use the ABCDE method to identify specific stressors, challenge automatic negative beliefs with evidence, and replace them with more accurate, functional thoughts.
- Consult a qualified mental health professional if stress persists for more than two weeks, interferes with daily functioning, or is accompanied by severe physical symptoms or thoughts of self-harm.

## Limitations
Reframing is not a substitute for professional medical or psychological care, especially in cases of trauma, severe anxiety, or physical illness. It does not eliminate the need to address unsafe work conditions or toxic relationships, and it should not be used to suppress genuine distress or avoid necessary action.
