## The short version
Dr. Amen uses SPECT scans to categorize ADHD into seven subtypes, arguing this improves treatment. While ADHD brain differences are real, his specific model lacks independent validation, and the high cost and radiation exposure of SPECT remain points of significant scientific and clinical debate.

## Why it matters
Understanding the distinction between established neuroscience and unvalidated clinical frameworks helps patients make informed decisions about expensive diagnostic tests. It highlights the ongoing shift in psychiatry toward biology-based diagnosis while emphasizing the need for rigorous evidence before adopting new clinical standards.

## Try this today
- Consult a qualified psychiatrist or neuropsychologist for a standard, evidence-based ADHD evaluation before considering expensive imaging options.
- Review the limitations of SPECT imaging, including radiation exposure and lack of insurance coverage, with a healthcare provider if pursuing brain-based diagnostics.
- Focus on evidence-supported lifestyle factors such as regular aerobic exercise, adequate sleep, and balanced nutrition, which have demonstrated benefits for ADHD symptoms regardless of diagnostic subtype.

## Limitations
The source is an informational article that presents both the proponents' and critics' views but does not constitute medical advice. The seven-subtype model is not recognized by major psychiatric associations, and SPECT imaging for ADHD is not a standard of care. Individual treatment responses vary, and this summary does not recommend specific medications or diagnostic tests.
