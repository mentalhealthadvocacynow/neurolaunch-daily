summary: "A laugh attack is a sudden, intense episode of uncontrollable laughter that often occurs at inappropriate times, such as during solemn events or high-stress situations. This phenomenon typically results from the brain's emotional control centers, specifically the limbic system and cerebellum, becoming overstimulated by tension, anxiety, or unexpected events. Unlike normal laughter, which is voluntary, a laugh attack is a reflexive response where the body's arousal levels exceed the prefrontal cortex's ability to regulate expression. While most laugh attacks are harmless nervous system responses to stress or trauma, they can occasionally indicate a medical condition known as pseudobulbar affect (PBA). PBA involves episodes of laughing or crying that do not match the person's actual mood and is linked to neurological conditions like multiple sclerosis, stroke, or brain injury. The source emphasizes that uncontrollable laughter is not a moral failing but a documented physiological mismatch between internal arousal and external expression. Common triggers include anxiety, sleep deprivation, and the contagious nature of hearing others laugh. While occasional episodes are normal, frequent or unexplained episodes warrant professional evaluation to rule out underlying neurological or mental health issues."

## The short version
Laugh attacks are involuntary bursts of laughter caused by high stress or neurological factors. While usually harmless, frequent episodes may signal conditions like pseudobulbar affect.

## Why it matters
Understanding the neurological basis of laugh attacks helps reduce shame and social anxiety associated with these episodes. It also highlights the importance of distinguishing between common stress responses and potential neurological symptoms that require medical attention.

## Try this today
- Practice slow, deliberate breathing and physical grounding techniques to help manage acute episodes of uncontrollable laughter.
- Consult a healthcare provider if laughter episodes are frequent, unexplained, or accompanied by other neurological symptoms such as muscle weakness or confusion.

The source is informational and does not provide a diagnosis. It notes that while most laugh attacks are benign, specific clinical conditions like pseudobulbar affect require professional medical evaluation. The article does not offer treatment plans for underlying neurological disorders.
