This high heritability indicates that inherited DNA accounts for most of the variation in ADHD symptoms within a population, though it does not imply a single 'ADHD gene.' Instead, the condition is polygenic, resulting from thousands of tiny genetic variations that each contribute a small amount of risk. These genetic factors interact with environmental influences, such as prenatal exposures or early life experiences, to determine whether symptoms manifest. No current blood test or DNA panel can diagnose ADHD; diagnosis remains a clinical process based on behavioral evaluation and history. While genetic research has identified specific loci and genes involved in dopamine signaling and brain development, these findings primarily help explain the biological basis of the disorder rather than providing a diagnostic tool. Understanding the genetic nature of ADHD helps dispel myths that attribute the condition to poor parenting or diet, reinforcing that it is a neurodevelopmental difference rather than a character flaw. Although genetic testing is advancing, particularly in pharmacogenetics to predict medication response, it is not yet accurate enough for individual diagnosis or treatment decisions."

## The short version
ADHD is highly heritable (70-80%) due to many small genetic variations, not a single gene. It results from a mix of genetics and environment. No genetic test can currently diagnose ADHD, which requires clinical evaluation.

## Why it matters
Recognizing ADHD as a biological condition with strong genetic roots reduces stigma and blame associated with parenting or lifestyle choices. It clarifies that the disorder is a difference in brain development, not a behavioral failure, and highlights the importance of professional clinical assessment over unverified genetic screening for diagnosis.

## Try this today
- Consult a qualified healthcare provider, such as a psychiatrist or psychologist, for a proper clinical evaluation if symptoms interfere with daily functioning.
- Review the original research articles to understand the specific genetic loci and environmental factors discussed in the source material.

## Limitations
The source is an informational article that summarizes existing research but does not provide individualized medical advice. Heritability estimates are population-level statistics and do not predict individual risk. Current genetic tests are not validated for diagnosing ADHD, and the relationship between specific genes and treatment response is still an area of active research with limited clinical application.
