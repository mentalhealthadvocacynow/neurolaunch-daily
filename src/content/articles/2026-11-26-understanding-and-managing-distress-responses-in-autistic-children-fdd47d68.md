## The short version
Aggressive outbursts in autistic children are usually distress responses to sensory or communication overload, not intentional aggression. Effective management involves reducing environmental triggers, using non-verbal communication tools, and avoiding punitive discipline during meltdowns. Professional help is recommended if behaviors escalate or cause injury.

## Why it matters
Misinterpreting distress as intentional violence can lead to punitive responses that worsen the child's anxiety and erode trust. Recognizing these behaviors as neurological responses allows caregivers to implement supportive strategies that reduce frequency and intensity, improving quality of life for both the child and family.

## Try this today
- Maintain a behavior log to identify specific environmental triggers and warning signs before episodes escalate.
- Reduce sensory input during outbursts by dimming lights, lowering voice volume, and creating physical space.
- Consult a pediatrician or developmental specialist if behaviors increase in severity, cause injury, or appear suddenly after a period of stability.
